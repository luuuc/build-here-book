The second time a problem comes back, that's information.

Volunteers take turns preparing the equipment for a workshop. Every time, something's missing, and only one person knows what. A short checklist, tried once and fixed, solves it for everyone.

A system is whatever makes next time easier: a checklist, a template, a rule, a tool, a person you've trained. It can be very small.

This section is about getting the dose right: delete a step before you document it, get knowledge out of a single head, write down what broke, and accept that not everything deserves to become a process.

**To start:** spot one thing you did twice this month. Write down how you do it.

**To go further:** look at the shortcut everyone takes. That's the real process. Bring the official one in line with it, or fix the shortcut.

**If you're growing a team:** give people time to pass things on. Knowledge that fits in one head is a problem waiting to happen, and rarely by that person's choice.

**A sign it's working:** the next person manages without you, and you can say what helped them.

**Next:** leverage, for when a solution deserves to go further.
