## The symptom

You are waiting on an access right, an answer, or a decision. The work cannot move as planned, and you do not yet know what next step to propose.

## The signal

Separate what you can check, the help you need, and the decision that belongs to someone else.

## What's going on

A blockage can be real: an authorisation is missing, one person holds the information, a tool is down. Making it visible helps you act on it. It proves neither a lack of initiative nor poor organisation on your part.

Some blockages shrink with a short search through documentation, an old case, or a known example. Others need help immediately. When you are starting out, not knowing where to look is already a reason to ask for direction. Cap the search according to the urgency, the risk, and what you already know. Twenty minutes can be a bearing for an ordinary question, never an entry fee for getting help. During an incident, or in front of an irreversible action, go to the competent person without waiting out that time.

A precise request makes answering easier: here is the intended result, the point where I am stuck, what I checked, and the effect on what follows. If you cannot make those checks, simply explain what is missing. You do not have to work around an access right or commit someone else without their agreement.

Sometimes the right decision is still to wait. In that case, name the next check-in date and the work that can move alongside. If nobody can answer, whoever owns the remit has to be able to reduce the commitment or push the deadline.

## Check this

On a current blockage, write:

> I'm trying to ... I'm stuck on ... I was able to check ... I'm missing ... The effect on the deadline is ...

Send the request to someone who can help or point you. Propose a moment to take stock, without promising their availability for them.

At that moment, check whether the blockage is cleared, whether other help is needed, or whether the commitment has to change. Keep a bearing that will be useful next time.

## From where you sit

- **Engineering**: tell a search you can do apart from an access that needs agreement.
- **Management**: make asking for help possible without requiring a minimum search time.
- **Customer relations**: explain where the file stands and the next agreed check-in.
- **Recruiting**: ask how the person looked for help, not only how they acted alone.
