## The reflex

> "This problem touches two teams. I don't know who to pass it to."

The difficulty is known, but following it up is nobody's job.

## The builder's reflex

> "I've spotted what's missing. Who can decide what happens next, and which part can I offer to take?"

## Why

Handovers between crafts can leave a need with no clear owner. A candidate is waiting to hear back after an interview. Recruiting thinks the manager writes; the manager thinks recruiting handles it. Flagging that hole with an example already makes it something that can be worked on.

You can then propose a bounded move: draft a message, look at a few files, or describe a fix. First check whether somebody is already on it, and what your proposal displaces. A problem with no visible owner can hide a decision, a risk or a workload you do not yet know about.

Your job description sets commitments. An initiative beyond them is a conversation with the people involved, especially if it takes time or changes a service. Helping once does not mean agreeing to own it forever.

On a first project the same principle holds: you notice someone struggling, you offer help, and the two of you agree what you will do. If the agreement, the time or the access is missing, a clear description of the problem is still a useful contribution.

## Try this

Pick a small problem and propose an intervention with a limit on it.

> "I can draft the message to candidates before Thursday. Who signs off the content, and who sends it afterwards? I'd need to push back the guide update."

Before you start, confirm those points with the people who can decide. After the attempt, check whether the need is covered and whether what comes next has an owner. Without agreement on what comes next, go back and discuss it rather than quietly picking the task up again.

## From where you sit

- **Design**: show the friction before changing a screen another team depends on.
- **Management**: arbitrate the load, and make explicit what the initiative does not commit anyone to.
- **Recruiting**: check who takes over with the candidate after each interview.
