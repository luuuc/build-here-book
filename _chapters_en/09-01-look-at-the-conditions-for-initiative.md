## The symptom

You would like to see more initiative around you. Some proposals never arrive, or go nowhere, and the reasons are not yet clear.

## The signal

Examine one concrete situation with the people concerned: goal, time, access, skills, mandate, and what happened to earlier proposals.

## What's going on

A repeated difficulty invites you to look at the working conditions. It does not prove everything comes from the organisation. An individual difficulty does not prove a hiring mistake either. The information available, experience, relationships and constraints can combine. Understand the case before assigning a cause.

A constructed example: several team members wait for approval to change a welcome sheet. Their manager thinks that decision is theirs; they have seen an earlier change reversed with no explanation. Clarifying the remit and the criteria could help. In another case, the blockage could come from a missing access or an already full workload.

If you lead the team, separate what you can change from what depends on another manager. If you are acting between peers, you can ask a question, make a constraint visible, or propose a trial. You do not have to redesign the organisation alone, or take a confrontation risk to show commitment. A private conversation, or the backing of an ally, may suit better.

A rule that changes does not necessarily produce an immediate effect. People may need explanations, practice, and to see how an initiative is received. Plan that support, and look at what already works too. Growing a team can mean spreading a good practice, rather than starting from a complaint.

## Check this

Choose a recent situation rather than a label on a person. Ask what was expected, what was possible, and what happened next.

With the people concerned, propose a bounded change: clarify a mandate, get an access, set aside time, or make a response more predictable. Escalate what goes beyond your role.

After a real chance to practise, look at whether the change helped and what is still hard. If the attempt could not happen at all, examine that constraint instead of concluding that people lack initiative.

## From where you sit

- **Engineering**: describe a concrete dependency before diagnosing a lack of autonomy.
- **Support**: bring a case where a proposal did, or did not, get a follow-up.
- **Management**: clarify your own remit and look for the backing you need elsewhere.
- **Recruiting**: separate learning needs, working conditions, and the criteria for the role.

## To discuss

Which condition would make a useful initiative easier, and who can help us change it?
