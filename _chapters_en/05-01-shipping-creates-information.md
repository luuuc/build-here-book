## The reflex

You have been preparing a change for three weeks. A question about use is still open. You carry on, hoping the answer will come at delivery.

## The builder's reflex

Choose what the next attempt has to teach you, then ship the smallest version that answers it.

## Why

Until someone uses it, you know nothing. Every week of work without feedback is a bet on your assumptions. Shipping turns the bet into information.

You are redoing the sales team's standard quote. Before sending it to every prospect, send it to two customers you know and ask them what they understand about the price. One day, and you find the line nobody reads.

A delivery with no observation teaches nothing. Decide beforehand what you will look at: a use, a number, a reaction. Otherwise you ship, move on to the next thing, and keep the same uncertainty.

Small does not mean without consequence. A single line can change a thousand records. For what cannot be undone, a migration, a commitment, a price, do a rehearsal on chosen cases first.

## Try this

On a change in progress, write:

> The question to inform: ...
> The smallest version that answers it: ...
> What we will look at, and when: ...

Ship it this week. Afterwards, separate what you saw from what you conclude. Carry on, change, or stop.

## From where you sit

- **Design**: choose an attempt that lets you observe an action, not only an opinion.
- **Operations**: prepare the rollback before changing the service.
- **Management**: set aside time to look at the result, not only to ship.
- **Recruiting**: ask what an attempt taught, without ranking candidates by frequency.

## To discuss

Which question does our next delivery have to inform, and how will we know whether it did?
