## The starting point

You are considering an AI tool for part of the work. The first result comes back fast, but what it is worth, how reliable it is, and the checking time are still to be examined.

## The move

Run a bounded attempt on a suitable task, with a quality reference and someone able to check the result. Compare the full effort, not only the generation.

## Why it works

A draft, a rewrite, or a proposed classification can help when you have a way of examining whether it is right. The choice depends on the task and its consequences. Fluent text is not enough to establish that it respects the facts, the context, or the recipient's needs.

If you are starting out, you can learn with a competent person, or on an exercise whose answer can be verified. Not having the skill to check an output is a limit to handle, not a reason to believe the result. If nobody can check an important part, shrink the attempt, find backing, or keep another method. Choosing not to use AI can be reasonable.

A team tries drafting a reply from an approved help sheet. They check that the draft keeps the conditions, promises nothing extra, and answers the case. They count the preparation, the review and the corrections, then compare to their usual method. That local result does not let them conclude anything about all their replies.

Check too whether the tool and the information it uses fit the working rules. To start, fictional cases or authorised content may be enough. The cost includes access, preparation, checking, rework, and follow-up if the practice lasts. A gain in output is only useful if the quality and the service still fit.

## Try this

Choose a low-consequence task, an expected result, and cases you are allowed to use. Define what has to be right and what would make you stop the attempt. With no access to a suitable tool, first compare the need to a method you already have.

Examine the outputs before they get used, and note the important errors as much as the total time. Do not mistake one small successful attempt for a general validation.

With the people concerned, decide to carry on, change, or stop. If you carry on, assign the checking and plan a review for when the tool, the data, or the task changes.

## From where you sit

- **Product**: check a classification against the original cases and their limits.
- **Design**: use a proposal as material to examine, not as evidence of use.
- **Management**: plan the access, the learning, and the checking time.
- **Customer relations**: check facts, conditions and commitments before using a draft.

## To discuss

Which task would allow a verifiable attempt, and what result would make us prefer our current method?
