## The reflex

You are preparing a change, but a question about use is still unanswered. The work carries on when you could perhaps check that question on a smaller version.

## The builder's reflex

Choose what the next attempt has to teach you, then prepare a version safe enough and useful enough to observe it.

## Why

Making work available creates an occasion to learn, not learning itself. Somebody has to be able to use it, the result has to be observable, and the feedback has to inform a decision. Several deliveries with no observation can leave the same uncertainty untouched.

A constructed example: you are helping a community group improve the directions to a workshop. Before redoing every invitation, offer the new text to a willing person who does not know the venue. Ask how they would plan their journey. That first attempt can reveal a missing piece of information; it does not yet prove that every participant will arrive without trouble.

A bounded change can make analysis and going back easier. That depends on its effects, not only on its size. A single line can change a lot of records. Check the scope touched, the protections needed, and who can authorise the attempt. If you are starting out, prepare it with someone who knows the context.

Some actions are hard to undo: a migration, an external commitment, a change to an essential procedure. You can sometimes reduce the uncertainty with a simulation, a rehearsal, or an examination of suitable cases before going live. An attempt does not replace the checks the final action requires, and piling up attempts does not justify exhausting the people who take part.

## Try this

On a change within your reach, write:

> The question to inform: ...
> The smallest useful attempt, its limits, and the agreement it needs: ...
> What we will observe, and when: ...

Plan the preparation and feedback time. After the attempt, separate observation from interpretation. Decide to carry on, change, check another way, or stop. If nothing conclusive was observed, write that down rather than turning the delivery into proof of success.

## From where you sit

- **Design**: choose an attempt that lets you observe an action, not only an opinion.
- **Operations**: prepare the conditions for going back before changing the service.
- **Management**: set aside time for the feedback and the decision that follows.
- **Recruiting**: ask what an attempt taught, without ranking candidates by frequency.

## To discuss

Which question does our next delivery have to inform, and how will we know whether it did?
