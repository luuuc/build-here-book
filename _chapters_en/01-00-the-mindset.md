The same question comes back after every sign-up. The answers help the people who receive them. Looking at what they ask, you notice that one line of the form can be read two ways.

You can start by checking that reading with someone affected. You can propose wording to whoever owns the form, or try a change yourself if it is yours to make. The first move depends on what you know and on what you can commit.

This capability gathers practices for learning from what surrounds you: asking a question, owning a mistake, taking criticism, proposing an improvement. They serve a first project as well as a craft practised for years.

Some conditions make them hard. A question can go unanswered. An initiative can add load to an already full week. Flagging a problem can be read as a commitment to solve it alone. Those conditions are open to discussion; the cards marked ⇄ address the people who can change them.

If you are starting out, pick something you can observe without changing anyone else's work. If you already have experience, start from a decision you would like to understand better, or from feedback you would like to make more useful. A request for help or a precise report can be the right outcome of this attempt.

You can see progress when a question brings information that was missing, when feedback moves your work, or when an initiative produces an effect you can describe. The result can also show why it is better not to step in.

**What to explore next:** the craft gives you tools to examine what you noticed. You can work on it in parallel, starting from the same problem.
