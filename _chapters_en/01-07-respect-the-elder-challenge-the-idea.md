## The reflex

An experienced salesperson proposes keeping an offer as it is. She knows the customers better than anyone. A new colleague has heard several requests that contradict that reading.

She keeps quiet: she is surely missing context. The decision goes through without her observations being looked at.

## The builder's reflex

> "I've heard three requests pointing the other way. What am I missing? Can we look at them before we decide?"

## Why

Experience brings what the newcomer lacks: the history, the exceptions, the forgotten reasons. Fresh eyes bring what experience no longer sees: what has changed. Neither wins on principle. The facts decide.

Respecting someone means taking their reasoning seriously enough to challenge it. Keeping quiet out of deference means letting them decide with one piece of information missing.

You do not need a solution to object. One fact that does not fit is enough. Put it forward with its possible consequence, and ask for the context you are missing. Either someone explains why you are wrong, and you have learned. Or the decision changes, and the team has avoided a mistake.

## Try this

On the next decision, prepare one fact, its possible effect, and a question. Say it before the decision is made, not after.

If you are running the discussion, say the objection back before you answer it. Note what was examined and why the choice holds.

If you are the experienced one, explain your reasoning and what would make you revise it. If you are the one deciding, ask for the objections before you give your own view.
