## What you are asking for

The team produces work that could be presented beyond its usual circle. What the contributors want, and how their part gets credited, are still to be discussed.

## What the system hears

Visibility can be an opportunity for some and unwanted exposure for others. A collective communication does not on its own say how their contributions are credited.

## What that produces

An organisation can choose a collective voice for practical or editorial reasons. Examine the case rather than automatically reading it as fear of losing staff. That form can still erase contributions if no other way of crediting them is planned.

Visibility can create meetings, feedback, or professional opportunities. It guarantees neither a hire, nor a departure, nor loyalty to a team. People choose on several conditions, some of them outside any manager's reach. Backing someone's development does not require promising they will stay, or using their exposure as a retention tool.

A constructed example: a team prepares a presentation of a joint project. Two people want to speak, another prefers a collective mention. They agree the content they may use, the contributions to cite, and the preparation time. The person not speaking is still credited for her work; she does not have to accept exposure to get the same professional backing.

If you decide on means, separate crediting the work, taking part in communications, and discussing a career. A public byline does not replace working conditions examined directly with the person. If you back this without that power, you can offer a review or an introduction both sides accept, without promising an audience or a promotion in exchange.

## The decision

On a piece of sharing under consideration, ask the contributors how they want to take part and be credited. Check the publication framework, the time needed, and the information that can circulate.

Agree the personal or collective mentions, who will answer questions, and the limits of that follow-up. Plan suitable credit for contributions that stay internal. Declining optional exposure must not be read as a lack of ambition or worth.

After the experience, ask what it brought, what it cost, and whether each person would do it again. Handle requests about role, load, or progression separately, with the people who can answer them.

## From where you sit

- **Management**: separate contributing to the work from wanting to speak about it.
- **Design**: have the content and the credits confirmed before presenting collective work.
- **Customer relations**: offer conversations that were accepted, without exposing a relationship by default.
- **Recruiting**: describe the real conditions and accept capability with no public visibility.

## To discuss

How do we offer visibility without imposing it, and how do we credit the people who would rather stay out of view?
