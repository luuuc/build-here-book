## What you are asking for

You want to follow the effects of the work. The team check-in mostly describes the tasks done and the ones coming, with no clear place for later observations.

## What the system hears

The format invites people to prepare activity. Adding results means saying which observations are useful, when they can arrive, and who can get them.

## What that produces

Following activity helps coordinate people and spot a load or a dependency. That tracking stays useful. It is not enough to know whether the work improved a situation, preserved a service, or reduced an uncertainty. Connecting the two lets you choose what comes next with more context.

The result does not always follow the meeting's cadence. A task can be finished this week and its effect only become observable at the next cycle of use. Forcing an immediate result can lead you to pick a convenient metric rather than relevant information. Note what is expected, what is still unknown, and when you plan to look.

Observations can be quantitative or qualitative: a case that used to be impossible, a difficulty explained, an incident avoided during an exercise, a procedure someone taking over can actually use. A measure needs context too. Few uses does not prove a rare service useless, and a rise in volume does not on its own demonstrate an improvement.

Preparing that feedback takes time and can need access. Agree a limited number of subjects to follow, with a person who accepts the check and the means to do it. If a result is disappointing or unknown, look for the next decision rather than for someone personally to blame. The team check-in has to help people act, not become a second job of reporting.

## The decision

On an important subject, add to the activity: the expected effect, the observation available, and the next check. Separate "not observable yet", "information out of reach", and "result observed".

Choose with the team who will gather the feedback, when, and with how much effort. Accept a sample check or a prepared account if direct access is not appropriate.

After a relevant cycle, ask which decision that tracking helped and how much time it cost. Simplify or stop tracking that no longer informs a decision, keeping the necessary controls.

## From where you sit

- **Product**: tie the observed result to a decision about what comes next.
- **Operations**: make the service preserved visible, even with nothing new.
- **Management**: fit the cadence and the means to the observations you expect.
- **Customer relations**: bring feedback with its context, inside the limits of what you may share.

## To discuss

Which result deserves a place in our review, and which tracking could we lighten to make room for it?
