## What you are asking for

People already explain, support and pass things on. You want to make that learning more reachable without adding an invisible load, or replacing useful help with a document.

## What the system hears

If passing on has to take a new form, somebody has to say who it is for, how much time it gets, what agreements it needs, and how you will know whether the form helps.

## What that produces

Mentoring can leave lasting capabilities with the people supported, who can then pass them on in turn. Its effect does not automatically stop when the mentor leaves. A written record can complete that work and make later access easier; it guarantees neither more readers nor better learning.

A colleague regularly helps prepare an interview with a member of the public. With the people she supports, she proposes a short sheet of questions and an annotated example. A peer tries the sheet, then explains where they still need help. The exchange improves the material without removing the conversations that are still useful.

The form depends on the need: pairing, a demonstration, an internal sheet, a group session, or public content when sharing fits and is allowed. Someone starting out can note what helped them; an experienced person can add the context and the limits. No title or length of service on its own carries a duty to publish for a whole profession.

Preparation, review, access and upkeep all need means. A lack of time can be real. Agree the work displaced and the information that can be shared before asking for new material. If you back this without budget authority, offer precise help or take the request to whoever decides. Recognition and credit are discussed with the contributors, with no publication quota and no exposure imposed.

## The decision

Choose a passing-on need together with the people it is for. Ask which form would help them and what already exists. Keeping the help spoken can be the right answer.

If new material is useful, plan a bounded effort, a willing or mandated owner, and a way to maintain it. For public content, have the sharing rights checked; an internal version is still a valid result.

After a trial, look at what people can now do or explain better and what help is still needed. Adjust, continue, or stop the material according to that feedback. A link circulated is not enough to show that anything was passed on.

## From where you sit

- **Engineering**: follow a demonstration with the bearings the recipient finds useful.
- **Product**: choose a passing-on need before choosing a format.
- **Management**: reserve preparation and upkeep inside the workload.
- **Support**: involve the people who use a sheet in keeping it up to date.
