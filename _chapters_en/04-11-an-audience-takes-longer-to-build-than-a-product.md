## The reflex

The launch is coming. The team finds it has no clear route yet to reach the first people concerned and gather their feedback.

## The builder's reflex

Set up that line while you build, through a channel that fits. A public audience is one possibility among several.

## Why

Trust and attention sometimes take several exchanges. How long depends on the subject, the people, the channel and the relationships that already exist. No publishing schedule guarantees that a group will be waiting for your launch. Early contact can inform the building. One person shows another way of solving the problem, asks a question about access, or agrees to a trial. That feedback can be useful before a solution is complete, provided you present honestly what already works.

Publishing can let you meet people beyond your circle. But a partnership, a private demonstration, a professional network or an internal handover may suit better. A service aimed at a few known users does not have to become a publication. Each route has a cost. Writing means preparing, reviewing, and sometimes getting approvals. Arranging conversations means finding slots and following up. Plan that time inside the project rather than adding it automatically to one person's evenings.

Choose what can be shared, too. Confidentiality, competition, or how far along the project is can justify a private trial or a wait. You can describe a need without exposing information that is not yours.

To start, one willing person and one precise question are enough. To go further, examine whether your current contacts cover the uses that interest you, or only the ones familiar to you.

## Try this

Choose a recipient, a channel, and a bounded effort: a conversation, a demonstration, or a piece of writing if that fits. Say what you want to learn and what you can show.

After that attempt, look at who was actually reached, what was understood, and what follow-up was accepted. Compare that effect to the time it took before you repeat, change, or stop.

The number of posts or followers is not enough to establish that a launch is ready.

## From where you sit

- **Product**: look for useful feedback while building, without promising a finished product.
- **Marketing**: compare several routes, including conversations and relays that already exist.
- **Management**: plan the preparation and follow-up time inside the workload.
- **Customer relations**: offer trials to willing people and organise what happens next.
