## The starting point

> "I sent the receipt link, and they were able to download it."

The answer did the job. A similar request comes back a few days later.

## The move

> "What makes that link hard to find?"

## Why it works

Settling a request and understanding what caused it are two different pieces of work. The first helps one person now. The second stops a hundred others from hitting the same difficulty.

Three receipt requests can come from a button that is hard to see, a message that never arrives, or three different situations. Look at what people did just before they asked. That is where the cause shows.

The same move holds for a job restarted after an error, a file sent back for a missing document, an instruction that has to be explained again. Every repeated fix is an inquiry waiting to happen.

Keep the effort proportionate. In an emergency, restore first. For an isolated case with no follow-on, move on. For a repetition that costs, give yourself an hour of inquiry.

## Try this

On a request that keeps coming back, write two sentences: what helped this time, and what might explain the difficulty.

Examine one case with the person concerned. Look for a case that contradicts your explanation too.

After a fix, watch the next occurrences. If the request comes back, go back to the cause instead of repeating the fix.

## From where you sit

- **Product**: compare the cases before turning a repetition into a general priority.
- **Management**: tell response time apart from the time an inquiry needs.
- **Support**: keep what the person was trying to do just before the difficulty.
