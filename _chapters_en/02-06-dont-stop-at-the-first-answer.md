## The starting point

> "I sent the receipt link, and they were able to download it."

The answer did the job. A similar request comes back a few days later.

## The move

> "What makes that link hard to find?"

## Why it works

Settling a request and understanding what caused it are two useful pieces of work. The first helps the person now. The second can stop other people hitting the same difficulty.

Three receipt requests can come from a button that is hard to see, from a message that never arrives, or from three different situations. Bundling them too quickly under one cause produces a badly aimed fix. Look at what the people did before they asked, and at what you can actually check.

The same approach holds for a job restarted after an error, a file sent back for a missing document, or an instruction that has to be explained again. A successful resolution gives the inquiry a starting point; it does not prove the cause is known.

The effort has to stay proportionate. An emergency may mean restoring the service first. An isolated case with no lasting consequence may not justify a long search. For a costly repetition, agree a time budget for the inquiry and a person to pass the facts to if the cause is beyond your access.

## Try this

On a request that keeps coming back, write two sentences: what helped this time, and what might explain the difficulty. Mark the second clearly as an assumption if you have not checked it.

Choose one case to examine with the person concerned. If you are starting out, read it through with someone who knows the flow. Look for a case that contradicts your explanation too.

After any fix, watch the next occurrences over a period that fits. If the request comes back, re-examine the cause instead of repeating the fix. You can close the request you served while keeping a separate inquiry with an owner and a date.

## From where you sit

- **Product**: compare the cases before turning a repetition into a general priority.
- **Management**: tell response time apart from the time an inquiry needs.
- **Support**: keep what the person was trying to do just before the difficulty.

## To discuss

Which recent resolution left us with a question about the cause, and what fact would let us examine it?
