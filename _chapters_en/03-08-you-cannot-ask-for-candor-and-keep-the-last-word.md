## What you are asking for

You invite objections before a decision. Few people answer, and it stays hard to know whether the plan is understood, accepted, or simply not examined yet.

## What the system hears

The invitation will be judged on what happens after an objection: examination, answer, any change, and the effects on whoever raised it.

## What that produces

Differences in power can make disagreement expensive. A person may fear for their appraisal, their relationship with the team, or their next chances at work. Silence can also come from a lack of time or information. So it is not enough to conclude either agreement or fear.

You can keep the responsibility for deciding and take an objection seriously. Listening does not oblige you to adopt every proposal. What is needed is being able to show what was examined, why the decision holds, or why it changes. An immediate, long answer from the person in charge can close the examination before other facts arrive. Taking the time to say the objection back, to check an assumption, or to come back in writing makes the exchange more useful. Not everyone needs to speak in front of the group.

Do not set a quota of concessions. Changing a plan to hit a number would be another way of not looking at the facts. Acknowledge the contribution with its author's agreement, especially if putting them forward could expose them.

To grow a team, protect what follows too: a good-faith objection must not become a mark against someone at appraisal time. If you have no authority over those rules, propose a channel and a frame to whoever answers for them, without promising protection you cannot provide.

## The decision

→ Before a call, say what is still open and offer a way to answer in writing or in private.
→ Examine an objection on its facts and its consequences. Give an answer, even when the decision stands.
→ After it is in practice, ask whether information was missing, and look at how the objections were handled.

Start with one bounded decision. Agree the time available for responses and the moment the decision will be explained.

## From where you sit

- **Management**: explain what an objection changed in the examination or in the choice.
- **Engineering**: use the channel that lets you lay out the facts without needless risk.
- **Customer relations**: bring the consequence for the user, with an example you can share.
- **Recruiting**: welcome the candidate's questions without reading them as a lack of interest.
