This case is **entirely constructed**. The people, the observations and the numbers are fictional. They show how to prepare and review an attempt, without promising the same result anywhere else.

Mariam takes part in a repair workshop at a community group. She does not organise the activity and has never run a project. She notices that newcomers keep asking where the entrance is. She offers bounded help to Idriss, who organises the welcome.

## Understanding before redoing

Mariam asks a willing participant to show her the invitation she received and to describe arriving. The participant found the building, but walked all the way round before spotting the right door. Mariam notes that case without concluding that every newcomer has the same trouble.

The current invitation says:

> Repair workshop, Saturday at 10am, community centre. Bring the object you want repaired.

Idriss confirms the workshop entrance is on the library side and that the other door stays locked on Saturdays. He also says the step-free access is through that entrance. Mariam does not need to collect information about participants' mobility to explain that route to everyone.

## The attempt sheet, filled in

- **Person helped:** a new participant planning their arrival.
- **Difficulty observed:** the invitation does not let you pick the right entrance.
- **Assumption:** a precise, confirmed direction will make the journey clearer.
- **Attempt:** have a version read by someone who does not know the venue, then use it for the next workshop if it works.
- **Agreement:** Idriss approves the information and keeps responsibility for sending it.
- **Effort available:** forty minutes for Mariam, fifteen of them for the review; ten minutes with Idriss. She drops preparing a poster this week to stay inside that limit.
- **Limits:** do not change the time, promise help nobody has organised, or circulate a volunteer's private contact details.
- **Stop:** do not send if the entrance or the time is still uncertain; ask Idriss for confirmed information.
- **Observation:** ask the reviewer to describe their route, then gather voluntary feedback at the welcome desk on Saturday.

The durations are the ones chosen in this case, not a universal budget for starting out.

## What changes after the first review

Mariam first writes: "Entrance on the library side." The reviewer asks whether you have to go through the library. She fixes that ambiguity with Idriss.

The version they keep becomes:

> Repair workshop, Saturday at 10am, community centre. From the square, take the path that runs along the library. The workshop door is at the end, on the left; that access is step-free. The main door on the square stays locked on Saturdays. Bring the object you want repaired. For a question before you come, reply to this message: the organiser will answer.

That last commitment is only added once Idriss confirms it. The text can be read on paper or in a message; it does not need an online map.

## The feedback and the next decision

On Saturday, Idriss asks three willing new participants how they found the entrance. Two describe the route with no trouble. The third had not seen the message and followed another participant.

Mariam and Idriss write this account:

> **Observed:** the two people we asked who had read the new text found the entrance. The first review let us fix an ambiguity.
>
> **Limit:** three responses do not represent every participant. We have no comparable measure from before this attempt. The third case raises a question about the message being received, not necessarily about the wording.
>
> **Decision:** keep the confirmed text. At the next workshop, Idriss will check separately how newcomers receive the invitation. No new project is automatically handed to Mariam.

## An explicit ending

Idriss agrees to keep the template and to check the directions before each send. Mariam hands him the text and the account; her help stops there. She does not have to watch every future invitation, or publish the story, for the work to count.

For your first attempt, keep the same logic with a need you can reach: one person, one question, one agreement, one bounded move, and one piece of feedback. If the first contact says no or has no time, look for another occasion that is accepted; a refusal is not a personal failure.
